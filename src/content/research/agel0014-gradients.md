For my M.Sc. thesis I measured the spatially resolved stellar populations of AGEL0014, a strongly lensed quiescent galaxy at z ≈ 1.4, to constrain how and when it stopped forming stars. Combining imaging from HST and Gemini/GSAOI with Keck/MOSFIRE spectroscopy, I modelled the lens with Lenstronomy.

Recovering an intrinsic gradient from a lensed, seeing-limited observation is the hard part. I forward-modelled source-plane metallicity gradients, applying light-weighting and PSF convolution to the projected image-plane map so the simulated data passed through the same slit as the real observation.

Full-spectrum fitting with ALF (Absorption Line Fitter) reveals a clear positive age gradient and a negative metallicity gradient. Together these support outside-in quenching: the core's high α-enrichment points to a short, intense burst that shut off abruptly, while the iron-rich outskirts imply a more extended star formation history with time for enrichment from Type Ia progenitors.

The analysis is complete and the manuscript is in preparation for the *Astrophysical Journal*.
