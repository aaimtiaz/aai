Having worked through the Sparkler and the wider CANUCS sample, the natural next question is where else this kind of measurement is possible. The Bullet Cluster is a promising hunting ground: a violent cluster collision, exceptionally well observed, with strong lensing that magnifies background galaxies enough to resolve individual star clusters.

The goal is to identify sparkler-galaxy candidates within the cluster, then perform photometry and SED fitting on them. Each new system adds a data point to the question of how star clusters' properties co-evolve with their host galaxies across cosmic time — a question that is currently answered from a very small number of objects.

I also produced Fitsmap visualisations of the Bullet Cluster, hosted on the CASSA website, so the data can be explored interactively rather than only through figures.
