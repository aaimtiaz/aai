Type Ia supernovae are the standard candles the distance ladder is built on: they explode at a characteristic brightness, so how faint one looks tells you how far away it is. Stacked far enough, they give the Hubble constant.

This project investigates the Hubble constant and — more interestingly — the *residual*: the scatter that remains once the standard corrections have been applied. Combining optical and near-infrared observations matters here, because the near-infrared is far less sensitive to dust extinction than the optical, so the two together separate "genuinely fainter" from "dimmed on the way".

It is the work that first brought me to Dr. Syed Ashraf Uddin, with whom I now also build the reduction pipeline for the CASSA observatory — an instrument aimed squarely at catching transients like these.
